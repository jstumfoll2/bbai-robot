\chapter{SENSOR FUSION: THE GPS EKF}
\label{ch:fusion}

The global estimate combines wheel odometry, the gyroscope, the compass and
GPS in an extended Kalman filter (EKF) from the robot\_localization package
\cite{moore2014generalized}, running on the laptop as node \texttt{ekf\_gps}
(\file{laptop/bbai\_slam/gps\_fusion.launch} and \file{gps\_ekf.yaml}). This
chapter states the filter's model, the measurement model of each sensor, and
how the result relates to the SLAM map.

\section{THE DISCRETE EKF}

The thesis gives the linear discrete Kalman filter in predictor--corrector
form (thesis \S7.7, after Kalman \cite{kalman1960} and Simon
\cite{simon2006optimal}). The EKF is the same recursion with the state
transition $f$ and measurement $h$ nonlinear and their Jacobians used for the
covariance:
\begin{align}
\xhat_{k}^- &= f\bigl(\xhat_{k-1}^+,\Delta t_k\bigr), &
P_k^- &= F_kP_{k-1}^+F_k\T + Q_k\,\Delta t_k, \label{eq:ekfpred}\\
\bm\nu_k &= \bm z_k - H_k\xhat_k^-, &
S_k &= H_kP_k^-H_k\T + R_k, \label{eq:ekfinnov}\\
K_k &= P_k^-H_k\T S_k^{-1}, &
\xhat_k^+ &= \xhat_k^- + K_k\bm\nu_k, \label{eq:ekfgain}\\
P_k^+ &= (I-K_kH_k)P_k^-(I-K_kH_k)\T + K_kR_kK_k\T, \label{eq:joseph}
\end{align}
with $F_k=\partial f/\partial\bm x$ at $\xhat_{k-1}^+$. The covariance update
\eqref{eq:joseph} is the Joseph form, the same one the thesis uses, which keeps $P$ symmetric and positive definite under round-off.
Angular components of the innovation are wrapped to $(-\pi,\pi]$.
robot\_localization runs the prediction at a fixed \SI{20}{Hz}
(\texttt{frequency}) and applies each sensor message as it arrives, after
predicting forward to the message's time stamp. Measurements may update any
subset of the state; $H_k$ is then the rows of the identity that select the
components marked \texttt{true} in that sensor's configuration vector.

\section{STATE AND PROCESS MODEL}

robot\_localization always carries a 15-element state,
\begin{equation}
\bm x = [\,x\ y\ z\ \ \phi\ \theta\ \psi\ \ \dot x_b\ \dot y_b\ \dot z_b\ \
\dot\phi\ \dot\theta\ \dot\psi\ \ \ddot x_b\ \ddot y_b\ \ddot z_b\,]\T,
\end{equation}
pose in the world frame and velocities and accelerations in the body frame.
With \texttt{two\_d\_mode: true}, $z$, $\phi$, $\theta$ and their derivatives
are fixed at zero, leaving the planar state
$[x,\ y,\ \psi,\ v_x,\ v_y,\ \omega,\ a_x,\ a_y]$. The process model is the
``omnidirectional'' constant-acceleration model:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ekfmodel}
\begin{aligned}
x_{k} &= x_{k-1} + \bigl(v_x\cos\psi - v_y\sin\psi\bigr)\Delta t
         + \tfrac12\bigl(a_x\cos\psi - a_y\sin\psi\bigr)\Delta t^2,\\
y_{k} &= y_{k-1} + \bigl(v_x\sin\psi + v_y\cos\psi\bigr)\Delta t
         + \tfrac12\bigl(a_x\sin\psi + a_y\cos\psi\bigr)\Delta t^2,\\
\psi_{k} &= \psi_{k-1} + \omega\,\Delta t,\\
v_{x,k} &= v_{x,k-1}+a_x\Delta t,\quad v_{y,k}=v_{y,k-1}+a_y\Delta t,\quad
\omega_k=\omega_{k-1},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with accelerations held constant between updates. Its Jacobian has the
familiar dead-reckoning entries
$\partial x/\partial\psi = -(v_x\sin\psi+v_y\cos\psi)\Delta t+\dots$ and
$\partial y/\partial\psi = (v_x\cos\psi-v_y\sin\psi)\Delta t+\dots$, the same
coupling as \eqref{eq:odomcov}. The process noise $Q$ is the package default
(a diagonal of small values, \num{0.05} on position and \num{0.06} on yaw per
second); it has not been tuned for this robot.

Written out, the measurement functions are linear selections,
\begin{equation}
h_{\mathrm{odom}}(\bm x)=v_x,\qquad
h_{\mathrm{imu}}(\bm x)=\begin{bmatrix}\psi\\\omega\end{bmatrix},\qquad
h_{\mathrm{gps}}(\bm x)=\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
so all of the nonlinearity is in the process model.

\paragraph{Design choices.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Odometry contributes speed only.} The odometry pose and its yaw
  are not fused. The pose covariance from \texttt{bbai\_base} is constant
  rather than growing (\S\ref{sec:odom}), so fusing it would over-trust it;
  and odometry yaw is the integrated gyroscope, which would double-count the
  gyroscope rate already fused from imu0. $v_y$ is not fused either; the
  model has no constraint that keeps it at zero, so it is held small only by
  the process noise. Fusing $v_y=0$ (a nonholonomic pseudo-measurement) would
  tighten the filter.
\item \emph{Heading is absolute from the compass, rate from the gyroscope.} The
  filter is then exactly the Kalman form of the complementary filter
  \eqref{eq:yawcf}: the gyroscope carries the heading between compass
  updates, and the compass bounds its drift. With variances of
  \SI{1e-4}{} on $\omega$ and \SI{0.05}{} on $\psi$ the compass corrects the
  heading slowly.
\item \emph{The gyroscope bias is not a state.} robot\_localization has no bias
  states, so a constant rate error appears as a heading ramp that the compass
  pulls back. The bias estimate comes from the base node's zero-velocity
  update instead (\S\ref{sec:zupt}).
\item \emph{No outlier gating.} The Mahalanobis thresholds
  (\texttt{*\_rejection\_threshold}) are left at their default of infinity,
  so a GPS multipath jump is accepted at its stated covariance. Setting
  \texttt{odom1\_pose\_rejection\_threshold} to about 3--5 (rejecting
  innovations with $\bm\nu\T S^{-1}\bm\nu$ above that many standard
  deviations squared) would guard against it.
\end{itemize}

\section{OBSERVABILITY AND BEHAVIOUR WITHOUT A FIX}

With a GPS fix the filter observes $x$, $y$ and $\psi$ directly and the
covariance stays bounded. Without one (always, indoors), $x$ and $y$ are not
observed: the filter integrates the odometry speed along the compass-and-gyro
heading, and $P_{xx}$ and $P_{yy}$ grow as in \eqref{eq:odomcov}. That is the
``coasting'' behaviour: \topic{/odometry/global} is then a dead-reckoning
estimate whose heading is anchored to the compass rather than to the gyro's
start-up direction. When fixes return, the first few updates pull the
estimate onto the GPS track with a jump proportional to the drift accumulated.

\section{RELATION TO THE SLAM MAP}
\label{sec:maprel}

The EKF publishes no transform (\texttt{publish\_tf: false}). Its output lives
in its own world frame \frm{gps\_map}, while gmapping owns
\frm{map}$\to$\frm{odom}. The two estimates run side by side:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
& SLAM (gmapping) & GPS EKF \\
\midrule
World frame & \frm{map} & \frm{gps\_map} \\
Absolute reference & the lidar map & GPS and the compass \\
Works indoors & yes & coasts only \\
Publishes TF & \frm{map}$\to$\frm{odom} & none \\
Consumers & RViz, map\_saver & topic only \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Both start from the same odometry, so for short runs they agree up to a fixed
rotation and translation. This keeps the two from fighting, but nothing yet
relates \frm{map} to \frm{gps\_map} or to latitude and longitude.

\paragraph{Aligning the frames.} If the robot drives a path outdoors where
both are valid, the rigid transform between them follows from the paired
positions $(\bm a_i,\bm g_i)$ in \frm{map} and \frm{gps\_map} by least squares
\cite{umeyama1991least}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:align}
\theta^\star=\atantwo\!\left(\sum_i \bar{\bm g}_i\times\bar{\bm a}_i,\
\sum_i \bar{\bm g}_i\cdot\bar{\bm a}_i\right),\qquad
\bm t^\star=\bar{\bm a}-R(\theta^\star)\bar{\bm g},
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\bm a}$, $\bar{\bm g}$ are the means and $\bar{\bm a}_i$,
$\bar{\bm g}_i$ the centred points ($\times$ is the planar cross product
$g_xa_y-g_ya_x$). Publishing $(\theta^\star,\bm t^\star)$ as a static
transform \frm{map}$\to$\frm{gps\_map} would put GPS fixes on the lidar map.
A fuller design, for when the robot navigates on a saved map, replaces
gmapping with a localiser (AMCL or slam\_toolbox in localization mode) and a
second, map-frame EKF that owns \frm{map}$\to$\frm{odom} and fuses both the
localiser's pose and GPS \cite{moore2014generalized}.
