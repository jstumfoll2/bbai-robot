\chapter{SYSTEM OVERVIEW}
\label{ch:overview}

\section{PURPOSE AND SCOPE}

This document records how the BeagleBone AI robot is built and how its
software fits together. Chapter~\ref{ch:platform} describes every piece of
hardware: the chassis, the BeagleBone AI, the Robotics Cape, each sensor, and
the power budget. Chapter~\ref{ch:hardware} records every change that was
needed to bring each device up on the board, and Chapter~\ref{ch:ros} shows
how the devices, computers and ROS nodes are connected. The navigation
chapters derive the wheel odometry (Chapter~\ref{ch:kinematics}), the motor
control and tracking loops (Chapter~\ref{ch:motor}), the attitude and
heading estimate from the gyroscope and magnetometer
(Chapter~\ref{ch:attitude}), the GPS measurement model
(Chapter~\ref{ch:gps}), the extended Kalman filter that fuses them
(Chapter~\ref{ch:fusion}), and the SLAM algorithms that build the map
(Chapter~\ref{ch:slam}). Chapter~\ref{ch:camera} covers the OAK-D-Lite
stereo depth camera and Chapter~\ref{ch:chase} the chase behaviour that uses
it to follow a dog or a person. Chapter~\ref{ch:modes} lists the operating
modes that are available with or without each sensor, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:issues} collects known problems, including the analysis of
the SLAM drift observed on 1 October 2026.

The notation and the sensor-error models are taken from the author's master's
thesis on the two-wheeled inverted pendulum (TWIP) \cite{stumfoll_twip}, in
particular its treatment of IMU measurements (thesis \S4.3.1), its sensor noise
model with a constant and a walking bias (thesis \S7.5), and its discrete
Kalman filter (thesis \S7.7). Where this document reuses a result from the
thesis it says so, so that the derivations can be read side by side.

\section{GOALS}

The goals, in Jason's priority order as of 1 October 2026, are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Back up and version all of the board's work (done: this repository and
      the \file{librobotcontrol} fork).
\item Chase the dog using on-camera detection on the OAK-D-Lite.
\item Map the house with lidar SLAM.
\item Drive manually with a PS4 controller over Bluetooth.
\item Publish the IMU, wheel odometry and GPS as ROS topics and fuse them into
      a single navigation state.
\end{enumerate}

\section{PLATFORM SUMMARY}

The robot is a small four-wheel skid-steer chassis. All four wheels are
driven, each by its own DC gear motor with a Hall-effect encoder. A BeagleBone
AI with a Robotics Cape is the only on-board computer. Table~\ref{tab:hw}
lists the hardware and Figure~\ref{fig:system} shows how it is connected.

\section{DIVISION OF WORK BETWEEN THE COMPUTERS}

The board runs everything that touches hardware: the ROS master, the lidar
driver, the motor, encoder and IMU node, the GPS node, the PS4 teleop node and
the chase node. The OAK-D-Lite runs its neural network on its own Myriad X, so
the board only receives detections. The laptop runs everything that is heavy
but not time-critical: SLAM, the EKF and navsat transform, and RViz. The
laptop joins the board's ROS master over Wi-Fi
(\texttt{ROS\_MASTER\_URI=http://192.168.3.120:11311}).

This split follows a decision made on 1 October 2026: keep everything on the
BeagleBone where possible, run SLAM on the laptop, and treat a Raspberry Pi~5
as the fallback compute if the board runs out of headroom.
